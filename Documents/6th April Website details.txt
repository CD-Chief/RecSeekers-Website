6th April Website details
The whole point of the website is to build our network. That’s it. Every page, every section, every bit of copy should push people towards leaving their details. The stronger the network, the stronger the business. That’s the core idea I want you to build around.

From a build point of view, this means CTAs need to be everywhere and consistent. Not aggressive, but always there. Things like “Join the Network” or “Get First Access”. The form needs to be simple, quick and low friction. First name, email, phone number, current role. That’s it. No long forms.

Once someone signs up, they’re in our ecosystem. On the front end, this should be positioned as value. They get first access to roles, market insight, PDFs, salary surveys and anything we release before it goes public. They’re also part of our monthly competition, giveaways, care packages etc, and will get the occasional email with useful content and updates. Keep this light and appealing, not salesy.

On the backend, it just needs to push cleanly into HubSpot and allow us to tag people depending on what they’re interested in. That part is important for us but doesn’t need to be explained on the site.

In terms of tone and presentation, everything should feel simple, confident and straight to the point. Not corporate, not overdesigned, not full of jargon. We’re positioning ourselves as a high quality, trusted network, not a loud agency.

On the EdTech side, I want you to understand the importance, but only show part of it on the site. EdTech is companies building tech for education, platforms, marketplaces, AI tools, things that are genuinely changing how the space works. It’s growing fast and we’re already working with some of the best emerging startups in the UK.

From a website point of view, don’t overload this. Keep it as a “coming soon” style section with a strong line about what it is and why it matters, plus a CTA to register interest. The goal isn’t to explain everything, it’s to capture people early and show where we’re going.

Same with the subscription model. Internally, this is a big play for us. Externally, it should be teased, not explained. Something like “Brand new subscription service launching Q3 2026 for priority clients” with a short line like “a new way of winning together”. No detail on pricing or structure. Just enough to create curiosity and drive enquiries.

On location and market focus, this needs to be really clear. We cover the whole of the UK. That’s the core market. Then we have a separate section specifically for people in Australia who want to move to the UK. We are not positioning moves from the UK to Australia anymore, so make sure that’s not implied anywhere.

Overall, the way to think about this is selective detail. You don’t need to say everything, you just need to say enough to build trust, show direction and get the user to act. Clean design, clear messaging, strong CTAs, low friction sign up.
